## Executive Summary

NILOC Technologies proposes to license and mature the U.S. Air Force's Pattern-of-Life Estimation Machine ("POLE Machine") — the AFRL Information Directorate (AFRL-RI, Rome, NY) framework protected by U.S. Patent 11,308,384 B1, "Method and framework for pattern of life analysis," and offered for license through AFRL-RI's technology-transfer office and TechLink — into CADENCE™, a deployable, accreditable activity-based-intelligence (ABI) analytics product. CADENCE models the rhythm of normal for an observed entity or population from historical, multi-modal ISR data — EO/IR imagery, Wide-Area Motion Imagery (WAMI), full-motion-video tracks, GPS/GMTI kinematics, and categorical and numeric attributes — and automatically surfaces the signal of change using POLE's three complementary detectors: statistical, spatial, and categorical anomaly detection.

The operational problem is acute and worsening: ISR analysts are drowning in WAMI and track data, while fielded anomaly tools are single-modality or purely numeric and cannot fuse imagery, geospatial, and semantic evidence into one normalcy model. POLE solves exactly this fusion problem — but as lab-validated research IP, it is not yet a hardened, real-time, analyst-facing product. Phase II closes that last mile.

Over a [24-month] effort, NILOC will re-implement POLE's validated core on a modern ML/MLOps stack, extend it with representation learning and calibrated ensemble detection, wrap it in an explainable human-in-the-loop analyst workflow, and demonstrate it at TRL 6–7 in a relevant operational environment with a DoD/IC transition partner. NILOC's edge is decisive: we are an AI-native small business — parent of the RFP Pipeline platform, with demonstrated depth in applied AI, large-scale data pipelines, and production MLOps — commercializing lab-validated Government IP, which materially lowers technical and schedule risk versus a clean-sheet build.

## Identification and Significance of the Problem/Opportunity

The ISR data deluge has outpaced human exploitation. A single WAMI sensor can persistently stare over a city-sized area, generating thousands of simultaneous tracks; add full-motion-video, EO/IR, GMTI, AIS, and multi-INT feeds, and the volume of behavior an analyst must observe vastly exceeds available exploitation capacity. The result is the well-documented PED bottleneck: sensors collect far more than humans can process, and consequential behavior — a vehicle deviating from its established route, a facility whose activity pattern shifts, an entity that suddenly associates with a watched network — is missed not for lack of data but for lack of attention.

Conventional anomaly tools do not fit the mission. Most fielded and commercial detectors are single-modality (imagery-only, or track-only) or purely numeric (thresholds on kinematics). They cannot reason jointly over the semantic, imagery, and numeric evidence that defines real behavior, and they generate false-alarm rates high enough that analysts disable them. What activity-based intelligence actually requires — and what tradecraft has demanded since ABI became a sustained DoD/IC thrust — is a normalcy model: a learned, per-entity and per-population baseline of what "normal" looks like across all modalities, against which deviations are scored and ranked.

AFRL already built the hard part. The POLE Machine framework (U.S. Patent 11,308,384 B1) constructs precisely this multi-modal normalcy model and flags deviation with three complementary detectors — statistical (distributional outliers in numeric/kinematic features), spatial (geospatial and trajectory deviation), and categorical (anomalous attributes and associations) — fusing EO, IR, WAMI, GPS tracks, and categorical/numeric data into one framework for activity-based intelligence. This is a validated solution to the fusion problem the commercial market has not solved.

The gap — and the opportunity — is productization. POLE is research-grade IP: proven in framework, not yet delivered as a real-time, explainable, accreditable, analyst-facing product deployable in DoD cloud (IL4/IL5) or at the disconnected tactical edge. NILOC's opportunity is to license the validated framework and close the last mile — modern ML performance, MLOps sustainment, human-in-the-loop workflow, and an ATO-ready deployment package — converting a strong Government research asset into a fielded force multiplier. Because the core is patented and validated, NILOC begins Phase II from a de-risked foundation rather than an unproven hypothesis.

## Technical Objectives

1. Faithful re-implementation and benchmark parity. License POLE and re-implement its three-detector framework (statistical, spatial, categorical) on a modern, scalable stack; demonstrate ≥ 95% detection parity with the AFRL reference behavior on a shared benchmark (WPAFB/CLIF WAMI, VIRAT, and a maritime AIS/xView3 set), with documented ROC agreement.
2. Performance uplift via representation learning. Extend the normalcy model with self-supervised imagery embeddings and sequence models for kinematic pattern-of-life; achieve a ≥ [30]% reduction in false-alarm rate at fixed probability of detection (Pd), or equivalently ≥ [0.05] AUC improvement, versus the re-implemented baseline.
3. Calibrated, explainable alerting. Deliver conformal-calibrated anomaly scores with per-alert attribution (which modality/detector drove the flag); hold the operating point to a verifiable false-alarm budget and produce an analyst-readable rationale for 100% of surfaced alerts.
4. Analyst-workload reduction (human-in-the-loop). In a structured human-subject evaluation with [6–10] representative analysts, demonstrate a ≥ 50% reduction in mean time-to-triage and a measurable increase in true-positive recall per analyst-hour versus the current manual/tools baseline.
5. Operational throughput. Sustain near-real-time performance on operational volumes — process ≥ [10,000] concurrent entity tracks and WAMI at [≥ 60] MP/frame at [2–4] Hz on a single accredited GPU node, with horizontal scale-out demonstrated.
6. Deployable, accreditable product at TRL 6–7. Package CADENCE as hardened, containerized software with full MLOps (drift detection, retraining, model registry), deployable via Platform One / Iron Bank toward a continuous-ATO path, and demonstrate it in a relevant operational environment with a named DoD/IC transition partner.

## Technical Approach & Work Plan

NILOC's approach is to preserve POLE's validated framework as the analytic core while replacing its research scaffolding with production-grade ML engineering, then wrap it in the workflow and sustainment layer that makes it fieldable. Work is organized into five tasks over [24 months], each closing at a milestone with an explicit go/no-go gate.

Task 1 — License, Baseline & Reference Re-Implementation (Months 1–5). Subtasks: (1a) Execute the AFRL-RI/TechLink license (field-of-use, with option to exclusivity) and complete technical transfer of the patented framework; (1b) stand up the reference data corpus (WPAFB/CLIF WAMI, VIRAT, AIS/xView3 maritime) with MISB/STANAG-4609 KLV-aware ingest; (1c) re-implement the statistical, spatial, and categorical detectors and validate parity against documented POLE behavior. Milestone M1: benchmark parity report. Go/No-Go: ≥ 95% detection parity before modernization proceeds.

Task 2 — Multi-Modal Normalcy Model Modernization (Months 4–12). Subtasks: (2a) self-supervised imagery/WAMI embeddings (ViT/DINO-style features) for entity and chip representation; (2b) sequence models (temporal transformers/temporal-conv) for kinematic pattern-of-life over GPS/GMTI tracks, with learned geospatial embeddings (H3 hex indexing); (2c) per-entity and per-population normalcy baselines via density estimation (GMM / normalizing flows) feeding the three detectors. Milestone M2: modernized model beats the Task-1 baseline. Go/No-Go: ≥ [30]% false-alarm reduction at fixed Pd, else revert to the parity baseline for that modality (safe fallback — no dead-end).

Task 3 — Calibrated Ensemble & Explainability (Months 8–15). Subtasks: (3a) score-level fusion of the three detectors via a learned meta-model with conformal prediction for a guaranteed false-alarm budget; (3b) per-alert attribution (SHAP-style) identifying the driving modality/detector; (3c) natural-language alert summaries generated under strict guardrails and injection-fencing — leveraging NILOC's applied-LLM depth — with every generated rationale traceable to underlying features (advisory only; the model never auto-adjudicates). Milestone M3: calibrated operating point + 100%-explained alerts. Go/No-Go: calibration holds on held-out data within [±20%] of target Pfa.

Task 4 — Analyst Workflow, HITL & MLOps Platform (Months 10–20). Subtasks: (4a) analyst UI for alert review, adjudication, and feedback, with active-learning loops that fold analyst decisions back into the normalcy model; (4b) MLOps — containerized services (Docker/K8s), model registry, feature store, automated drift detection and retraining, data/version lineage, CI/CD; (4c) deployment engineering toward IL4/IL5 on Platform One with Iron-Bank-hardened images and a continuous-ATO (cATO) path, plus a disconnected/DDIL edge build. Milestone M4: deployable product build + human-subject evaluation. Go/No-Go: ≥ 50% triage-time reduction in the analyst study.

Task 5 — Operational Demonstration, Test & Transition (Months 16–24). Subtasks: (5a) integrate with a transition partner's data environment (e.g., a DCGS/CDAO-aligned enclave); (5b) throughput and scale test against Objective 5 targets; (5c) operational demonstration and TRL 6–7 assessment with the transition sponsor; (5d) Phase III / transition agreement package. Milestone M5: TRL 6–7 demonstration report + signed transition intent.

Risks & Mitigations. License/IP-transfer delay → begin TechLink negotiation at award with a pre-agreed term sheet; CRADA fallback keeps engineering unblocked. Labeled-data scarcity → self-supervised pretraining, synthetic augmentation, and Government-furnished data reduce dependence on labels. False-alarm rate → conformal calibration plus HITL feedback bounds it by design. Latency at scale → streaming architecture, GPU acceleration, and model distillation. Accreditation timeline → build on Platform One/Iron Bank from Day 1 so the ATO path is engineered in, not bolted on.

Defense/IC transition. The natural first customer is the AFRL-RI sponsor itself, with expansion to CDAO/Maven, NGA, DIA, Army INSCOM/PEO IEW&S (DCGS-A), SOCOM, and the Combatant Commands — all of whom operate the WAMI/FMV/multi-INT feeds CADENCE is built for. Dual-use commercial markets. The same normalcy-plus-anomaly engine generalizes cleanly to smart-city/traffic analytics, physical security and critical-infrastructure protection, commercial maritime domain awareness (ports and shipping), fleet and logistics optimization, and insider-threat/fraud detection. Because CADENCE is pure software, productizing for these verticals is fast and capital-light.

Competition & differentiation. CADENCE competes with full-stack platforms (Palantir, Anduril) and point video-analytics vendors (BriefCam, Avigilon-class tools). NILOC differentiates on three axes: (1) a lab-validated, patented multi-modal normalcy framework rather than a proprietary black box, (2) AI-native execution at small-business cost and speed, and (3) explainable, calibrated, analyst-first alerts that survive contact with real operators.

Revenue model & IP strategy. Revenue combines subscription SaaS, term software licenses for Government on-prem/edge deployment, deployment/integration services, and a managed PED-as-a-service offering. On IP: NILOC licenses POLE from AFRL-RI (field-of-use, option to exclusivity) while owning the modernization, workflow, and MLOps layers as new NILOC IP, and asserts SBIR data rights on Phase II deliverables — a structure that protects both the Government's equity in the base patent and NILOC's investment in the productized system.
