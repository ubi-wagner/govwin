## Executive Summary

Small unmanned aircraft systems (sUAS) have become the defining asymmetric air threat of the decade — cheap, autonomous, and increasingly fielded in coordinated swarms against airbases and forward operating bases, borders, airports, stadiums, prisons, and critical infrastructure. Today's counter-UAS (C-UAS) sensors are overwhelmingly single-modality: radar-only systems miss low-RCS and hovering drones; RF-only systems go blind against autonomous, RF-silent, or frequency-agile drones; and cloud-hosted analytics add latency the engagement timeline cannot afford. Almost none reliably separate friend from foe inside a mixed swarm.

NILOC Technologies will field AURA™ (Airspace Understanding, Recognition & Alerting), a low-C-SWaP edge appliance and embeddable software layer that fuses passive RF-signature analysis, EO/IR, radar, and acoustics through a multi-modal deep neural network to detect, classify, and perform per-drone Identification-Friend-or-Foe — including across simultaneous swarms — with all inference running at the tactical edge. AURA is built on a Government-owned, lab-validated invention (U.S. Navy patent US 12,461,538, NSWC Crane) that NILOC will license and mature into a hardened, fielded product rather than reinvent.

NILOC's edge is an AI-native execution model paired with a license-from-validated-IP strategy that retires core technical risk before Phase II begins. We request [$1.9M] over [24 months] to re-host the DNN onto modern NVIDIA Jetson Orin-class hardware, expand its threat-classification library, add cooperative allow-list IFF and TAK/C2 integration, harden the appliance, and demonstrate a [TRL 6–7] prototype in a relevant/operational environment. Transition runs back through the originating Navy lab and the Joint Counter-sUAS Office (JCO), with immediate dual-use pull from airports, correctional facilities, stadiums, and critical-infrastructure security — where lawful detect-and-classify (as opposed to restricted kinetic mitigation) opens a broad, near-term commercial market.

## Identification and Significance of the Problem/Opportunity

The commoditization of capable sUAS has inverted the traditional cost-exchange ratio of air defense. A $500 quadcopter or $200 analog FPV drone can surveil a base, drop contraband into a prison yard, shut down a major airport, or — in growing numbers — arrive as one of dozens in a coordinated swarm. Adversary and non-state use of first-person-view and autonomous drones in recent conflicts has demonstrated that the threat is no longer emerging; it is operational, evolving monthly, and outpacing the fielded sensor base.

The core technical failure of current C-UAS is single-modality brittleness. Radar struggles with the low radar cross-section, low altitude, and near-zero Doppler of small hovering rotorcraft, and it saturates against clutter. Passive RF detection is powerful against radio-controlled drones but is defeated the moment a drone flies a pre-programmed GPS route with its downlink silent, hops frequencies, or hides inside ambient Wi-Fi/LTE. EO/IR gives identity but needs a cue and fails in weather and darkness. Acoustics are short-range. Each modality has a complementary blind spot, yet most fielded systems bolt sensors together at the display level rather than fusing them at the decision level — so confidence stays low, false-alarm rates stay high, and operators are flooded with ambiguous tracks.

Two harder gaps compound this. First, swarms: legacy pipelines report "multiple contacts" but cannot resolve, track, and individually classify each drone in a mixed formation. Second, friend-or-foe discrimination: on a modern range or installation, friendly ISR drones, first-responder platforms, and hobbyist aircraft share the airspace with threats, and a system that cannot tell them apart is operationally unusable and legally fraught. Finally, latency and connectivity: cloud inference is untenable at a contested FOB or a hardened site, so recognition must happen at the edge, at low size, weight, and power.

The opportunity is exceptional. Counter-sUAS is among the most heavily and durably funded lines in the DoD portfolio, coordinated across the Services by the Joint C-sUAS Office, with repeated live down-select and demonstration events and an explicit "family of systems" acquisition model that favors interoperable, sensor-and-software components exactly like AURA. Running in parallel is a large civil market — DHS/CISA critical-infrastructure protection, CBP borders, airports, the Bureau of Prisons, and stadium/mass-gathering security — where detection and classification are broadly lawful even where active mitigation is restricted to specific federal authorities. The Navy's NSWC Crane invention already proves the hardest part — multi-modal fusion with per-drone IFF at the edge — is feasible. Phase II's job is to mature, harden, and transition it.

## Technical Objectives

1. License and re-baseline the NSWC Crane multi-modal DNN, then port and optimize it from the legacy NVIDIA Jetson TX2 onto a modern Jetson Orin-class module, sustaining [≥15 fps] concurrent four-modality inference at [≤25 W] module power (a target [8–10×] AI-throughput headroom over the TX2 baseline).
2. Expand the classification library to [≥50] current drone classes and variants — including analog FPV, digital/frequency-hopping, Wi-Fi/LTE-piloted, and RF-silent autonomous platforms — achieving [≥95%] probability of detection and [≥90%] classification accuracy at [≤1 nuisance alarm/hour] on a held-out test set.
3. Mature swarm handling to simultaneously detect, track, and classify [≥20] concurrent drones in a mixed formation, with per-track friend/foe assignment at [≥90%] accuracy.
4. Implement cooperative IFF (FAA Remote ID ingest, friendly-asset transponder/allow-listing, and geofencing) to reduce nuisance-alarm rate by [≥50%] in cluttered electromagnetic environments.
5. Integrate with C2 via TAK/Cursor-on-Target and a documented API, publishing tracks to a common operating picture and demonstrating slew-to-cue of at least one representative EO/IR or third-party effector.
6. Field and validate a hardened AURA Sentry prototype at [TRL 6–7] in a relevant/operational environment, with a defined RMF/ATO and Phase III transition path.

## Technical Approach & Work Plan

Our approach de-risks the program up front by licensing a Government-validated invention, then matures it along four vectors — hardware modernization, model/data expansion, fusion/swarm/IFF robustness, and productization/transition — on a spiral cadence with quarterly demonstrations aligned to Government T&E interests.

Task 1 — License Acquisition, Baseline Re-Host & System Architecture (Months 1–5). Subtasks: (a) execute the field-of-use license through NSWC Crane T2 / TechLink (Navy Express License) and establish a CRADA/data channel with the inventing lab; (b) reproduce the published TX2 baseline and instrument accuracy, latency, and power; (c) port to a Jetson Orin-class module with TensorRT INT8/FP16 optimization; (d) define the AURA system architecture, sensor interfaces, and a requirements baseline (SRS). Deliverable/Milestone: baseline-reproduction report + architecture/requirements review. Go/No-Go: baseline reproduced within [±5%] of published metrics and Orin port sustains [≥15 fps] four-modality inference at [≤25 W]. Risk/Mitigation: license timeline → engage T2 pre-award and parallel-path on the public patent disclosure plus simulation until execution.

Task 2 — Data & Classification-Library Expansion (Months 3–14). Subtasks: (a) assemble a current sUAS corpus of RF downlink/telemetry captures plus paired EO/IR, acoustic, and radar signatures across [≥50] platforms; (b) retrain the RF-as-image spectrogram object-detection CNNs and per-modality classifiers; (c) augment with synthetic/GAN-generated RF for rare and adversary types; (d) explicitly address the RF-silent/autonomous threat by re-weighting cross-modal evidence toward EO/IR, radar, and acoustics. Deliverable/Milestone: model-performance report on held-out data. Go/No-Go: [≥95%] Pd and [≥90%] classification accuracy at [≤1 nuisance alarm/hour]. Risk/Mitigation: scarcity of adversary-drone data → controlled range collections, synthetic spectrograms, partner datasets, and transfer learning.

Task 3 — Fusion, Swarm Tracking & IFF Engine (Months 6–17). Subtasks: (a) implement decision-level (mid) fusion with graceful degradation when a modality is denied or jammed; (b) multi-target track formation with per-track classification across simultaneous drones; (c) cooperative IFF ingesting Remote ID, friendly transponder allow-lists, and geofences. Deliverable/Milestone: fusion/swarm/IFF test report. Go/No-Go: track and IFF [≥20] simultaneous drones at [≥90%] per-track accuracy; [≥50%] nuisance-alarm reduction from allow-listing. Risk/Mitigation: urban EM clutter false alarms → multimodal confirmation, adaptive thresholds, cooperative allow-listing.

Task 4 — Appliance Engineering, C2 Integration & Hardening (Months 10–20). Subtasks: (a) package the AURA Sentry fixed/portable appliance and the AURA Core embeddable SDK (the classification-software layer for third-party C-UAS stacks); (b) integrate sensors and demonstrate slew-to-cue; (c) implement TAK/CoT plus a documented REST/gRPC API to C2/COP; (d) environmental/EMI hardening to [MIL-STD-810/461]-representative levels and cybersecurity/RMF artifacts toward ATO. Deliverable/Milestone: appliance bench + integration acceptance. Go/No-Go: appliance passes integrated bench test, publishes valid CoT tracks to a TAK client, and cues a slave sensor. Risk/Mitigation: integration/ATO drag → standards-first (TAK), modular interfaces, and early RMF engagement.

Task 5 — Field Demonstration, TRL 6 Validation & Transition (Months 16–24). Subtasks: (a) demonstrate against a scripted mixed-threat/swarm scenario at a partner range or representative site (airport, correctional-facility, or FOB-representative); (b) capture T&E data with JCO/Crane engagement; (c) deliver the Phase III transition, ATO, and commercialization roadmap. Deliverable/Milestone: field-demonstration report + Phase III transition package. Go/No-Go: demonstrated [TRL 6–7] performance meeting objective thresholds in the field → Phase III readiness. Risk/Mitigation: transition funding gap → sole-source Phase III pathway, JCO alignment, licensing-lab sponsor, and parallel commercial revenue.

## Related Work & Prior Results

The foundational innovation for AURA was conceived and reduced to practice by U.S. Navy researchers at NSWC Crane Division and is protected by U.S. Patent 12,461,538 ("as represented by the Secretary of the Navy"). The Government's work demonstrated the decisive result this program builds on: a multi-modal deep neural network that fuses passive RF-signature analysis — using object-detection CNNs applied to RF spectrograms, treating emitters as "objects" in a time-frequency image — with EO/IR, radar, and acoustic inputs, performing per-drone friend-or-foe discrimination across simultaneous drones, and doing so at low C-SWaP on an embedded NVIDIA Jetson TX2. That constitutes Government-validated feasibility of the highest-risk elements. NILOC did not invent this core capability and does not claim to; our contribution is to license it and mature it — modern edge hardware, an expanded and current threat library, robust swarm/IFF and denied-modality behavior, cooperative allow-listing, C2 integration, hardening, and field validation.

The approach is well-grounded in the broader literature on RF-fingerprint-based sUAS detection, spectrogram CNN classification, and the JCO's live test-and-evaluation events, which repeatedly show that multi-sensor fusion outperforms any single modality. NILOC's own prior results establish execution capability rather than a competing C-UAS system: NILOC operates an AI-native model for maturing federally developed innovations, and is the parent of RFP Pipeline, a production AI-native federal-proposal platform — direct evidence of the applied-AI, edge-inference, and systems-integration competencies this Phase II requires.

## Commercialization Plan

Market. Counter-UAS is a large, durable, dual-use market. We estimate a global TAM of [$4–6B (2024) growing to $12–15B by 2030, ~[18–24%] CAGR]. The SAM relevant to AURA — the detection/classification sensor-and-software segment, excluding kinetic effectors — is roughly [$1.5–2.5B]. A realistic five-year SOM for NILOC is [$25–40M], captured across defense program revenue and dual-use commercial sales. (All figures are [estimates] pending a Phase II market study.)

Defense transition. The natural path runs back through the technology's origin: NSWC Crane and Navy C-UAS sponsors, then the Joint C-sUAS Office family-of-systems enterprise, whose interoperable, standards-based acquisition model fits AURA's TAK-native, open-integration design. Additional customers include Army PEO C-sUAS, USSOCOM, and Department of the Air Force installation defense. A sole-source Phase III — funded by a program office or through the licensing lab — is the primary defense off-ramp, de-risked by JCO-aligned T&E data generated in Task 5.

Dual-use commercial. A crucial legal reality widens the commercial aperture: active drone mitigation is restricted to specific federal authorities, but detection and classification are broadly lawful. AURA detects, classifies, and cues — it does not itself defeat — so it can be sold today to airports, the Bureau of Prisons and state corrections (contraband-drop interdiction), stadiums and mass-gathering venues, energy/utility and data-center critical infrastructure, and border operators, either as the AURA Sentry appliance or the embeddable AURA Core software layer.

Competition & differentiation. Established players include DroneShield, Dedrone (Axon), Fortem, Anduril, Citadel Defense, SRC, and Robin Radar. Most are single- or dual-modality, perform fusion in the C2/cloud, or ship closed effector stacks. AURA's differentiators are concrete: decision-level fusion of four modalities with per-emitter DCNN IFF, executed entirely at the edge at low C-SWaP; an open software layer that augments rather than replaces existing C-UAS investments; and a Government-validated IP base that materially lowers technical risk versus clean-sheet competitors.

Revenue model. Three streams: (1) appliance hardware sales; (2) recurring software licensing/subscription, including a "signatures-as-a-service" threat-library update feed — new drone models pushed continuously, the way endpoint security ships new signatures — which creates durable ARR; and (3) integration and Government program revenue.

IP & licensing strategy. NILOC will negotiate a field-of-use / co-exclusive license to the Navy patent as background IP, respecting the Government's retained government-purpose rights and Bayh-Dole obligations. NILOC will develop and own the foreground IP — the Orin re-host and optimization, the expanded/retrained classification library, the fusion/swarm/IFF architecture, cooperative allow-list IFF, and TAK integration — protected by NILOC patents and trade secrets, securing a defensible commercial position layered on a lawful federal foundation.
